# Pilot proposal

A 12-week pilot at the anti-rabies clinic of Government Medical College (GMC) Jammu, run by its Community Medicine department. Add one more clinic if the Health department confirms one. Nothing here is agreed yet. This page says what we would need, what we would measure and which decisions are open.

## What the pilot tests

1. Do reminder calls in Dogri, Hindi and Urdu help patients finish the course, compared with patients at the same clinic who get the clinic's usual care?
2. Can patients' spoken bite places be matched to wards well enough to be useful?
3. Is the city-wide notched share among biting street dogs near the 78 to 80% coverage JMC and the Chief Minister have stated, or well below it, once the vets' sight-counts show how far biting dogs differ from the dogs seen? Which areas to look at first needs more answers than 12 weeks at one clinic give (see [What the volume allows](#what-the-volume-allows)).
4. Does a sick-dog alert reach a vet the same day, is it right, and does the vet act on it?

It does not test whether bites go down. 12 weeks is too short for that.

## Clinics

- **GMC Jammu anti-rabies clinic.** The only clinic confirmed to give anti-rabies injections in Jammu division, from the National Rabies Control Programme list updated 26 March 2026. Intradermal vaccination has run there since about 2011-12.
- **A second clinic, if confirmed.** The Directorate of Health Services Jammu would choose it, ideally one that serves peri-urban or rural patients. We have not found any public source that says which other hospitals run anti-rabies days or which schedules they use. Candidates in our data are marked unverified.

## What each side provides

**Health and the clinic:** staff time at the desk (the enrolment form is built to take under 15 seconds, not yet timed with real staff); a sticker on the bite card (the app prints 8 to an A4 sheet at `/clinic/sticker`, once the missed-call number is set; the Dogri and Urdu lines need a native reader first); a reviewer; the previous quarter's register counts as the baseline; written answers to the decisions below.

**JSCL and JMC:** a vet to receive alerts; sight-counts in a few wards; confirmation of ward names and of the five wards whose recorded area looks wrong (wards 7, 62, 72, 73 and 74); ward population for the 75 current wards if it exists.

**DC Jammu:** the order, and a decision on who the sender of automated calls is.

**The team:** the software, a host, the missed-call number set-up with the provider, prompt audio, a weekly digest, and a 100-case check of the place matcher.

## How question 1 is tested

A before-and-after comparison cannot show the effect of the calls: season, staffing and vaccine stock change between quarters. So the pilot compares two groups at the same clinic in the same weeks.

- **Allocation.** The register number decides, before the desk knows anything about the patient. Even numbers are offered the missed-call enrolment and the reminders (arm A). Odd numbers get the clinic's usual care and are not offered it (arm B). Parity is used, not alternate days, because the kinds of patient who come differ by weekday. The desk writes the arm next to the register entry. Nothing is withheld that the clinic does today.
- **Who is counted.** Every new patient allocated to an arm, whether or not they gave the missed call. The result is therefore the effect of offering the service. We also report the share of arm A who enrolled.
- **Outcome.** A completed course means all doses the schedule requires were marked given by clinic staff on the register by the end of week 12. Doses a patient says on the phone they took ("patient says taken" in the app) are reported on their own and never counted towards completion. Both arms are read from the same source, the clinic's dose marks.
- **Enrolment closes at the end of week 8**, so the last patient reaches day 28 by the end of week 12.
- **Size.** To tell 60% completion from 70% (two-sided 5% level, 80% power) needs about 350 patients per arm, 700 in all. That assumes the full 10-point rise applies to everyone allocated to arm A. If only half of them enrol and the rise is limited to those who do, the difference between the arms is about 5 points, and about 1,470 per arm would be needed, which one clinic will not reach in 8 weeks. The 60% and 70% are the Puducherry figures; the GMC baseline is not known. The previous quarter's register counts tell us how many new patients to expect. If fewer than about 700 are expected in 8 weeks, we say so before starting and either add the second clinic or lengthen the pilot. We do not shrink the comparison.
- **Stopping rule.** Written down before week 1. One look at the data, when half of the target in each arm has passed its last scheduled dose date. Stop early only for harm or a very large difference (p below 0.001 either way), or at once if the clinic reports a reminder call that told a patient something unsafe or wrong. Otherwise the pilot runs to week 12. A weak or null result is reported as it is.
- **Approval.** The allocation needs an ethics committee's approval before week 1. Which committee is a decision below.

The before-after comparison stays as a second, caveated number: arm A against the same clinic's register for the previous quarter, with the season, staffing and stock differences stated next to it.

## What the volume allows

Every figure here comes from three planning numbers: 700 new patients in the 8 enrolment weeks (the size above; the previous quarter's register counts replace it before week 1), shared between the arms by register number, and the 50% enrolment target (metric 2).

| Step | Patients | A week |
|---|---|---|
| New patients allocated | 700 | 88 |
| In arm A, offered the service | 350 | 44 |
| Enrolled, at the 50% target | 175 | 22 |

Only enrolled patients answer, and only on the first call, so answers stop when enrolment closes at the end of week 8. The 175 is a ceiling for the answers about the dog's ear: the patient still has to answer the call, agree to the four questions, have been bitten by a street dog, have seen the ear and have been bitten inside JMC. We have no Jammu figure for any of those. The made-up history in the demo has 12 to 24 in 100 who could not see the ear. The two lower columns are illustrations, not forecasts.

| Usable notch answers | 175 (ceiling) | 105 | 70 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 95% interval either side of a 70% share | 7 points | 9 points | 11 points |

**What 12 weeks can show.**
- **The city-wide notched share, roughly.** At the ceiling, a measured 70% comes with an interval of 63 to 76%, so a share well below the claimed 78 to 80% can be told from it. A gap of a few points cannot.
- **Whether the pipeline works:** completion in arm A against arm B (metric 1), how many enrol, how many places are matched, whether an alert reaches a vet the same day.

**What it probably cannot show.**
- **Which areas to look at first.** There are 8 areas. At the ceiling that is 22 answers in an area on average, under the 25 needed to show a share at all. A "look here" flag needs 25 answers in the area and 25 in the rest of the city within the latest 4 full weeks, and enrolment gives at most 88 answers in any 4 weeks, about 11 an area. No area can be flagged, and after week 8 the latest 4 full weeks hold no first calls, so the flags are empty at the end of week 12. The area page says how many more answers each area needs. Flagging areas would need at least 50 usable answers a week (8 areas by 25, over 4 weeks), which is 2.3 times the planned 22 even if every enrolled patient gave a usable answer, and about 4 to 6 times with the losses in the table.
- **A change in coverage, or in bites.** That needs months, not 12 weeks.
- **How often sick-dog alerts happen.** The pilot can show whether one reaches a vet and is acted on, not how common they are.

## Before week 1

- A native speaker has read every prompt text, and native recordings replace the machine voices.
- Counsel has looked at the privacy design and the consent wording.

- The server is set up in pilot mode (below), with real keys and the first administrator. No demo data exists in pilot mode.

### Setting up the server (pilot mode)

Set these before the first start. The server refuses to start without them and says which one is missing.

- `CT_MODE=pilot`.
- `CT_SECRET_KEY`: at least 32 characters, random, kept secret. It signs the logins. Make one with `python3 -c 'import secrets; print(secrets.token_urlsafe(48))'`. A shorter key is a start-up error.
- `CT_PHONE_KEY`: the key that locks the phone numbers (a Fernet key, see `.env.example`). Lose it and the numbers cannot be read; leak it and anyone with the database can read them.
- `CT_PUBLIC_URL`: the address people reach the server at, such as `https://reminders.example.org`. The phone page's QR code and the browser's security rules use it, and the server sends the HTTPS-only header when it is `https`.

Nobody can sign in to a new pilot server, so the first administrator is made on the server itself:

```bash
uv run chaar-teeke create-admin
```

It asks for a username and a password (at least 10 characters, typed twice, not shown, never printed or logged), writes one audit row that says the command line made the login, and refuses to run again once any administrator exists. Every other login is added in the app, on the administrator's People tab. The administrator sets the system up and reads the audit log; they do not read any clinic's patients.

## Metrics

All are for the 12 weeks and for the pilot clinics only.

1. **Course completion**, arm A against arm B, from clinic-marked doses only (see above). This is the headline number, with its 95% interval. The patient-reported doses are shown separately. The before-after comparison with the previous quarter is a secondary number.
2. **Enrolment rate:** the share of arm A, the patients offered the service, who give the missed call. Target: 50% or more. This is our target, not an evidence-based figure.
3. **Notched share, city-wide.** Among street-dog bites inside JMC, where the patient could tell, the share with the right-ear notch. Shown with a 95% interval, the number of answers, and the number who could not see. It is compared with JMC's 78 to 80%, as a sign of where to look and not as a measure of coverage. Only arm A patients make calls, so the answers come from them: at most 175, and the interval is then about 6 to 7 points either side of the share, wider with fewer (see [What the volume allows](#what-the-volume-allows)). With fewer than 25 answers the page says how many more are needed and shows no percentage.
4. **Location quality:** the share of enrolled bites with a ward set, the share of enrolled bites inside JMC limits, and the matcher's accuracy on 100 counter-checked cases. That check needs 100 spoken places, which is 57% of the 175 enrolled at the ceiling. If fewer arrive, the check uses all of them and reports how many there were.
5. **Alerts:** sick-dog alerts sent, claimed by a vet and closed with an outcome. For each we record the delay from the clinic visit (day 0 of the course, the only bite date we hold) to the alert, from the alert to the vet's first sight, and from the alert to the close. Report the median and the slowest. Report **alert precision**: of the alerts the vet closes, the share the vet confirmed as a suspect animal. The vet writes "suspect confirmed", "not suspect" or "dog not found" as the first words of the outcome note. An alert carries only ward, day and place type, and the pilot does not add a landmark.
6. **Areas.** The area page runs on the pilot's answers, and the pilot reports how many answers each area reached and how many more it needed. At the planned volume no area can be flagged "look here" (see above). Twelve weeks is too short to show a change in coverage.

Illustration, not a forecast: if all 350 patients in arm A enrolled and the Puducherry effect (a rise of about 10 points) held for them, about 35 more would finish the course. If half enrolled and the rise held only for them, about 18 more (175 by 10 points), the 5-point difference between the arms that the size above allows for. That study used manual calls from staff, in one rural centre, with no control group. We have no Jammu baseline. The pilot measures it.

## Calibrating the notched share

Before reading the share in metric 3, the vets count street dogs in 2 to 3 wards over the same weeks: notched, not notched, unsure, as recorded in the app. Two numbers are compared: the notched share among dogs seen, and the notched share among bitten dogs from the answers in the same area. If they are close, bites track the dogs on the street and the share is a fair sign of coverage. If the bitten share is lower, biting dogs are less often notched than dogs seen, and the share read alone understates coverage, or sterilised dogs bite less. Both are reported with their intervals and with the number of "unsure". An area with too few answers shows no share, as everywhere else. The calibration says nothing about wards that were not counted.

## Costs

- **Telephony: unknown.** Automated calls to real patients must go through a registered sender, on a 1600-series number or the government route, not through a consumer SIM. We have no quote. To find out: (1) ask Health and Medical Education which provider it or the 104 or 108 call centre already uses; (2) ask that provider, in writing, for the price per outgoing minute, the monthly number rent, the set-up and registration steps and time, and whether calls from Health count as service, transactional or government calls; (3) ask the provider whether missed calls to a 1600-series number can be received; (4) ask how many calls at once it allows. Volume to quote on: about 4 to 5 calls per patient (one first call and one reminder before each later dose). The machine-voice prompts of a first call add up to about 2.5 to 3 minutes if every question is heard in full, and the greeting and notice (about 65 seconds) cannot be skipped. A reminder call runs about 30 seconds. Both are on top of the time the patient takes to answer. Add retries (up to 3 attempts per call) and morning-after calls for unmarked doses. See [TELEPHONY.md](TELEPHONY.md).
- **Hardware: for the demo only.** A SIM dongle (Rs 5,799 in our October 2026 research; not re-checked), a SIM, and a Linux machine to hold it. The pilot does not use them.
- **Hosting:** a free tier or a government host. Not decided.
- **Other:** stickers, and the time of the voice speakers. No new clinic staff are needed if the desk marks doses while the patient is there.

## Risks

- **Ownership crosses departments.** The clinics belong to Health, the sterilisation team to JSCL. The order is what removes this risk. It is the biggest one.
- **Place matching on real speech is unmeasured.** Our numbers come from machine voices ([EVALS.md](EVALS.md)). We expect many places to go to review at first. That load is for the reviewer.
- **Notch recall is imperfect, and biting dogs are not a sample of dogs.** The share is a sign of where to look first, not a census and not a measure of coverage. The vets' sight-counts are how to calibrate it (see above).
- **Telephony rules.** Using the wrong route risks the sender's services being barred. Get the route confirmed first.
- **One voice channel per dongle.** Fine for the demo. A pilot needs a provider with several channels.
- **Children and people without phones.** Guardian consent is needed in person. A relative's phone can be enrolled, but that is not tested.
- **Vaccine out of stock.** A clinic can switch "vaccine out", and reminders then say to call the clinic first. It depends on staff using the switch.
- **Vets may not act.** Their agreement to act on alerts is not confirmed. The pilot should not start without it.
- **Ward data.** Ward names differ between sources for a few wards. Ward population exists only for the old 71 wards. See [DATA-LICENCES.md](DATA-LICENCES.md) for sources.
- **Bite figures conflict.** Assembly replies give figures that do not add up across periods. We use only the 76,824 figure for two years.
- **Machine voices.** Dogri and Urdu texts are machine drafts. Do not go live with them.

## Decisions needed

**From GMC Community Medicine and Health:**
1. Will Health act as data fiduciary, and who signs the processor contract?
2. Which schedules are enabled? We recommend only the intradermal Updated Thai Red Cross (days 0, 3, 7, 28) and the intramuscular Essen (days 0, 3, 7, 14, 28). The clinic chooses for each patient. The software never does.
3. After a late dose, the system proposes no new dates. Later reminders keep the original calendar and the late patient is listed for the clinic, which gives the next date by its own protocol. The patient is only told to call or come to the clinic. Please confirm in writing.
4. Who marks a patient as adult or guardian consented, and how is a child's guardian consent taken?
5. How long are course records, call logs and bite reports kept?
6. Who is the place reviewer and who is the complaints contact?
7. Can the previous quarter's register counts be shared as the baseline?
8. Is there a second clinic?
9. Which ethics committee reviews the allocation by register-number parity, with the clinic's usual care for the odd numbers?

**From JSCL:**
1. Will a vet claim and act on sick-dog alerts, and within what time?
2. Will the team do sight-counts of notched dogs in 2 to 3 wards, to calibrate the share?
3. Can JMC confirm the ward names and areas flagged in our data notes, and supply ward population? Is there a local sterilisation monitoring committee that should receive the area view?
4. Is a minimum of 25 answers before showing a percentage acceptable?

**From the Deputy Commissioner:**
1. Will the DC convene and issue the order?
2. Who is the named sender of the automated calls: Health, JSCL or the clinic? The provider contract and the number registration depend on this.
